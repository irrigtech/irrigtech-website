What Three Weeks of Early Warning Is Worth

A hypothetical scenario: a phosphorus drop caught weeks before it would show in the plant, and what the response would look like.

Field Scenario · 21 Jun 2026 · 6 min read

This is a hypothetical, illustrative scenario, not a record of an actual IrrigTech deployment — IrrigTech does not yet have a track record of pilots to report on. It is written up because the pattern is a common one in continuous-monitoring literature: the value of the system would not be a dramatic save, it would be three weeks of notice.

The block

Roughly four hectares, mixed vegetables, on a sandy loam that drains fast and does not hold phosphorus well. Two sensor nodes in the root zone reporting moisture, NPK and pH every ten minutes, plus a flow meter on the supply line.

What the data would show

Through early spring the phosphorus trace would sit in the low-mid range, flat. In the third week it would begin to slope down — not a cliff, a steady decline of a few percent per reading across several days. Moisture and pH staying steady would rule out the common distortions. The trend line, not any single reading, is what would trigger the alert.

On this soil and this crop, a phosphorus slide like that would typically reach visible symptoms — dull colour, stalled growth in the youngest leaves — in about three weeks. By then the deficiency would be established, the correction would take longer to act, and the growth lost in the meantime would be gone. A grower's normal check might be a lab test every few weeks; the timing of the next one could easily miss the start of the slope entirely.

What the response would look like

A modest phosphorus application through the fertigation line, split over two irrigations, could start the day after the alert. The trace would be expected to flatten within a week and recover to the earlier range over the next two, with no visible symptoms appearing. The cost would be a bag of fertiliser and ten minutes at the controller.

There would be no clean before-and-after yield number, because the whole point is that nothing visible happens. What the block gains is optionality: a cheap, early, unhurried correction instead of a late, larger one made under pressure with the crop already behind. Continuous readings are not valuable because they catch disasters — those are rare and usually obvious. They are valuable because they turn slow problems into early ones, and early problems are cheap.

Educational article. Examples, figures and scenarios are general or illustrative — not results from IrrigTech deployments. IrrigTech's hardware is at prototype and early-deployment stage.
